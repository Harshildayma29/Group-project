# Design Thinking: Finding Parking Spaces

## Problem statement
**How might we help people quickly find available parking spaces in crowded areas?**

## 1. Empathize
People driving bikes and cars sometimes have to search for parking near shopping areas, hospitals, colleges and offices. This can waste time and fuel. Interview 4–5 people in your college or family and record their answers before presenting interview findings.

Questions to ask:
- Where do you find parking most difficult?
- How much time do you usually spend searching?
- Would seeing available spaces in advance help?

## 2. Define
Drivers have difficulty knowing whether a parking location has available spaces before they reach it. Bike and car drivers also need separate capacities and prices.

## 3. Ideate
Possible solutions:
- Search locations by area
- Display available and total spaces separately for bikes and cars
- Show estimated price
- Save a booking and cancellation to a database
- Future: verified live data and map navigation

## 4. Prototype
Create a small website with:
- HTML: page structure
- CSS: simple responsive design
- JavaScript: filtering and API requests
- Python built-in web server: receive requests and save bookings
- SQLite: store parking places, users, and booking history

## 5. Test
Ask classmates to use the prototype and complete these tasks:
1. Find bike parking in Vastrapur.
2. Check available car spaces in CG Road.
3. Register and create a booking.
4. View and cancel the booking.
5. Check whether the database was updated.

Collect real feedback such as: what was easy, what was confusing, and what to improve. Do not claim interviews or tests happened until you actually complete them.

## Suggested future improvements
- Map and GPS integration
- Sensors or parking-attendant updates for real availability
- QR code check-in
- Proper secure payment gateway
- Trained predictive model once there is historical usage data
